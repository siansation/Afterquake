        How the forecast at your spot works

        1. How many. Reasenberg–Jones aftershock model fitted to events at or before the selected time. This prototype uses one prior everywhere; USGS operational forecasts use regional parameters and more detailed completeness handling.

        2. Where. Past epicentres are source proxies with a 6 km distance smoothing term and fixed 10 km depth. This is a simplified spatial model.

        3. How hard. The Allen–Wald–Worden (2012) active-crust intensity equation, including scatter, integrated over a normalised M5–8.5 magnitude distribution. Smaller aftershocks and sources beyond 400 km are excluded.

        4. Your ground. A soil term fitted to ShakeMaps: ΔMMI = × log10(Vs30/760).

        5. Your building. Damage threshold from the Modified Mercalli scale, or for "typical local building", from the USGS PAGER casualty model for that country. It drops one level if this spot already shook at that level, or if the ground-failure model gives at least 10% liquefaction.

        6. Simulated futures. 200 Monte Carlo runs from the same posterior and shaking model as the formula. Their frequencies vary because the sample is small. This Reasenberg–Jones implementation does not model secondary triggering (ETAS).

        7. Planned exposure. Integrate the aftershock rate over your selected 5-minute to 24-hour interval. The projected start is when that whole interval falls below your chosen limit, assuming the model continues unchanged.

        8. Compare places. Both pins use A's effective intensity threshold, the same replay time, duration and shaking equation. Only location-dependent shaking inputs differ. The pins are not verified shelters or building assessments.

        9. Saved checks. One check per earthquake sequence stays in this browser. The explanation compares replay times, known catalogue entries, locations, assumptions and data/model versions. It does not fetch live warnings or news.

        Replay limits. The catalogue contains later revisions, the mainshock ShakeMap may have been processed years later, and the soil coefficient was fitted using these sequences. This is a retrospective illustration, not a real-time hindcast or independent validation of local shaking.

        Prototype only, not safety advice. Whether a building is safe to enter is decided by a structural inspection and local authorities.

        Does it forecast how many aftershocks?

        At 0.5–60 days after each mainshock, forecast regional M5+/M6+ occurrence over the next 7 days using events whose origin times precede that moment. Uses a revised catalogue; overlapping windows are not independent. This does not validate local shaking or building damage.

        Does it predict how hard they shake?

        The felt-report equation compared with USGS ShakeMaps of 26 real aftershocks. A negative gap means the equation predicted stronger shaking.
